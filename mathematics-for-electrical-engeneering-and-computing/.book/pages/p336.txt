                              Equations with three unknowns
                              For three unknowns we need three axes to represent the equations.
                              Each equation is represented by a plane, for example, Figure 13.17 shows
                              the plane which represents the equation x + y + z = 1.
                                 Two planes, if they intersect, will intersect along a line and if a third
                              independent equation is given then the three planes will intersect at a
                              point. More than three unknowns cannot be represented geometrically.
                                 However many unknowns there are in a system of equations, the three
                              types of systems which we identified as Cases 1–3 remain as do the
                              methods to be used to distinguish between a determined, indeterminate,
                              and inconsistent system.
