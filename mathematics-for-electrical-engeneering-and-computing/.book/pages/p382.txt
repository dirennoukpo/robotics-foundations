                                         Case (1): Real distinct roots y = A eλ1 t +B eλ2 t . Notice that λ1 λ2 must
                                                   be negative when a, b, and c are positive and therefore the
                                                   complementary function will die out as t → ∞ . In this case
                                                   the system is said to be overdamped.
                                         Case (2): Complex roots y = ekt (A cos(ω0 t) + B sin(ω0 t))
                                                   where k = −b/2a which is negative, so ekt is a negative
                                                   exponential term. This then represents dying oscillations. The
                                                   system is said to be underdamped.
                                         Case (3): The roots are equal, then = k = −b/2a and y = (At + B)ekt
                                                   Again this has a negative exponential part causing the com-
                                                   plementary function to tend to zero as t → ∞. This case is
                                                   referred to as critical damping.
